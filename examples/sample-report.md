# The Impact of AI on Renewable Energy in 2025

## Executive Summary

In 2025, the intersection of artificial intelligence (AI) and renewable energy is defined by a dual dynamic: escalating computational power demands paired with sophisticated operational enablement of clean energy systems. While AI infrastructure requires substantial electricity, it simultaneously acts as an accelerator for renewable deployment through corporate procurement, predictive generation forecasting, smart grid management, and energy storage optimization. 

---

## Market Valuation and Growth Trajectory

The integration of artificial intelligence into clean energy infrastructure has created a rapidly expanding market. Driven by investments in grid management, generation forecasting, and asset optimization, the global market for AI in renewable energy is estimated at **$1.06 billion in 2025** [1]. Over the next decade, this market is projected to reach **$9.27 billion by 2035**, expanding at a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 24.32% [1].

---

## AI Energy Demand and Corporate Clean Power Procurement

The computational workloads underpinning modern AI models require significant power:

* **Global Electricity Footprint:** In 2025, AI-focused data centers consumed approximately **155 TWh** of electricity, representing roughly **0.49% of total global electricity generation** [2].
* **Clean Energy Power Purchase Agreements (PPAs):** To offset growing energy requirements and meet decarbonization targets, technology companies have become the primary drivers of renewable energy contracts. In 2024, Big Tech accounted for **43% of all clean energy PPAs** signed globally [3].

---

## Utility Adoption and Grid Modernization

Electric utilities are rapidly turning to digital solutions to manage the increasing complexity of balancing renewable generation with consumer demand:

* **North American Utility Deployment:** A 2025 survey of electric utility executives found that **81% of North American utilities** have adopted AI, data analytics, and grid-edge intelligence to oversee and manage their distribution networks [4].
* **Network Stability:** Utilities are leveraging these tools to monitor transmission systems, integrate distributed energy resources, and prevent outages.

---

## Mitigating Intermittency: Generation Forecasting and Energy Storage

The intermittent nature of wind and solar resources presents a challenge for grid operators. AI technologies address this volatility through improved predictive capabilities and storage management:

* **Commercial Generation Forecasting:** Specialized forecasting providers are broadening satellite-enhanced, AI-driven wind and solar generation predictions. In 2025, providers such as Spire Global expanded these AI forecasting services into major, fast-growing energy networks, including the Electric Reliability Council of Texas (ERCOT), to enhance grid reliability [5].
* **Battery Energy Storage Optimization:** Machine learning algorithms are increasingly deployed to optimize battery energy storage systems, enhance battery operational efficiency, predict demand surges, and stabilize smart grids [6]. Regionally, **China leads the deployment** of large-scale, AI-optimized energy storage and smart grid projects [6].
